\section{Resource-normalized measurement and the bi-factor law}
\label{sec:model}

We first fix the measurement protocol, then state the bi-factor law and its
identifiability theorem. Estimation details follow the corollaries.

\subsection{Definitions and assumptions}

If $\n$ validators share a host of $Q$ cores, each receives $Q/\n$ cores.
Measured throughput then mixes consensus cost with a shrinking compute budget.

\begin{Def}[Resource-normalized measurement]
\label{def:rnm}
A campaign follows the \emph{\RNM{} protocol} with quota~$\q$ if every validator
container receives the same CPU quota and memory limit independently of~$\n$,
with aggregate reservation $\n\q$ below host capacity. Normalized throughput is
$\TPSn\eqdef\TPS/\q$.
\end{Def}

\begin{Assumption}[Separable compute budget]
\label{as:separable}
On the operating range of quotas in the campaign,
$\TPS(\n,\cc,\q)=\q\cdot\Phi(\n,\cc)\cdot(1+r)$ with $|r|\le C\q$ for a
constant~$C$ independent of~$(\n,\cc)$ there. Quota enters multiplicatively up
to a relative error controlled by~$\q$ --- a bound on a fixed interval, not an
asymptotic claim as $\q\to0$.
\end{Assumption}

Assumption~\ref{as:separable} is expected only within a restricted quota
regime (CPU-bound verification under cgroup quota; away from GC-dominated
small~$\q$, bandwidth saturation, or fixed block periods).
Section~\ref{sec:exp} tests it via Statement~\ref{st:qinvariance}. If rejected
on the full range, subsequent estimates are reported only where invariance
approximately holds --- a conditional quota interval, not a universal law.

\begin{State}[$\q$-invariance]
\label{st:qinvariance}
Under Assumption~\ref{as:separable} and
$\Phi(\n,\cc)=\Tzero\,\gfun(\cc)\,\n^{-\beta}$, the OLS slope of
$\log\TPSn$ on $\log\n$ at fixed~$\cc$ does not depend on~$\q$.
\end{State}

\begin{Proof}
$\log\TPSn=\log\Phi+\log(1+r)$ with $|r|\le C\q$; at fixed~$\q$ the remainder is
absorbed by the intercept, so the slope equals $-\beta$ for every such~$\q$.
\end{Proof}

\begin{Def}[Bi-factor scaling law]
\label{def:bifactor}
Under the \RNM{} protocol,
\begin{equation}
\TPS(\n,\cc)=\Tzero\cdot\gfun(\cc)\cdot\n^{-\beta},
\quad
\gfun(\cc)=\frac{\cc^{\gamma}}{1+(\cc/\csat)^{\theta}},
\label{eq:bifactor}
\end{equation}
with $\Tzero>0$, $\beta>0$, $\gamma\in(0,1]$, $\theta>\gamma$, $\csat>0$.
In the unsaturated regime $\cc\ll\csat$ this is log-linear:
$\log\TPS=\log\Tzero+\gamma\log\cc-\beta\log\n$.
\end{Def}

The form separates concurrency from validator-count scaling, reduces to a
power law for $\cc\ll\csat$, saturates at large~$\cc$, and contains the
classical single-factor law as the case $\gamma=0$ (or $\lambda=0$ with
$\alpha=-\beta$). Here $\beta>0$ encodes replication; $\theta>\gamma$ makes
$\gfun$ unimodal.

\subsection{Identifiability}

The next theorem is the main theoretical result. Changing the experimental
concurrency schedule changes the observed scaling exponent even when the
protocol itself is unchanged.

\begin{Theorem}[Identifiability of the consensus exponent]
\label{thm:identifiability}
Let observations follow the unsaturated bi-factor law with additive noise of
mean zero and finite variance, along a concurrency path
$\log\cc_{i}=\lambda\log\n_{i}+\mu+\eta_{i}$
($\E[\eta_{i}\mid\n_{i}]=0$). If the empirical second moment of
$x_{i}=\log\n_{i}$ converges to a positive limit, the OLS slope $\ahat_{m}$ of
$\log\TPS_{i}$ on $x_{i}$ alone satisfies
$\ahat_{m}\xrightarrow{\Prob}\gamma\lambda-\beta$.
In particular $\ahat>0\iff\lambda>\beta/\gamma$, and $\ahat=-\beta\iff\lambda=0$.
\end{Theorem}

\begin{Proof}
Write $x_{i}=\log\n_{i}$, $y_{i}=\log\TPS_{i}$. The path reduces the unsaturated
law to $y_{i}=a+\alpha x_{i}+\varepsilon_{i}$ with $\E[\varepsilon_{i}\mid x_{i}]=0$,
finite variance, and
\begin{equation}
\alpha=-\beta+\gamma\lambda.
\label{eq:biasdecomp}
\end{equation}
Centred OLS gives
$\ahat_{m}=\alpha+N_{m}/D_{m}$ with
$N_{m}=\sum(x_{i}-\bar x)\varepsilon_{i}$ and
$D_{m}=\sum(x_{i}-\bar x)^{2}$.
The second-moment assumption yields $D_{m}/m\to\Var^{*}(x)>0$; the summands of
$N_{m}$ are uncorrelated, mean zero, and of bounded second moment, so
$N_{m}/m\to0$ in probability. Continuous mapping:
$\ahat_{m}\xrightarrow{\Prob}\alpha$. Signs follow from $\gamma>0$.
\end{Proof}

Thus $\alpha=\underbrace{-\beta}_{\text{protocol}}
+\underbrace{\gamma\lambda}_{\text{measurement}}$:
a positive fit can reflect a scaled load generator rather than improving
consensus. Only $\lambda=0$ recovers~$-\beta$.

\begin{Remark}
With $\gamma=0.8$, $\beta=0.5$ and $\lambda=1$ one obtains $\ahat=+0.3$.
Extrapolating from $\n=5$ to $\n=60$, the two exponents differ by a factor
$\approx7$, matching the order of the discrepancy in~\cite{peniak2026a}.
\end{Remark}

\begin{Corollary}[Non-transferability]
\label{cor:sign}
Laboratories measuring along $\lambda_{1}\ne\lambda_{2}$ obtain exponents
differing by $\gamma(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})$. Without reporting~$\lambda$,
published single-factor exponents cannot be interpreted as protocol properties.
\end{Corollary}

\subsection{Implementation}

On a factorial grid ($\lambda=0$) we estimate $(\log\Tzero,\gamma,\beta)$ by
OLS in log-space and $(\csat,\theta)$ by nonlinear least squares on the full
$\cc$-sweep (Section~\ref{sec:exp}). The calibrated law then feeds prediction
intervals and the sizing program of the next section.
