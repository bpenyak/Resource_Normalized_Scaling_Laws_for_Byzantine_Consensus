\section{Resource-normalized measurement and the bi-factor law}
\label{sec:model}

\subsection{The \RNM{} protocol}

If $\n$ validators share a host of $Q$ cores, each receives $Q/\n$ cores, so
measured throughput mixes growing consensus cost with a shrinking compute
budget. An exponent fitted to such data is a property of the host, not of the
protocol.

\begin{Def}[Resource-normalized measurement]
\label{def:rnm}
A campaign follows the \emph{\RNM{} protocol} with quota~$\q$ if every validator
container receives the same CPU quota and memory limit independently of~$\n$,
with aggregate reservation $\n\q$ below host capacity. Normalized throughput is
$\TPSn\eqdef\TPS/\q$.
\end{Def}

\begin{Assumption}[Separable compute budget]
\label{as:separable}
$\TPS(\n,\cc,\q)=\q\cdot\Phi(\n,\cc)\cdot(1+O(\q))$ on the operating range:
the quota enters multiplicatively and does not interact with~$\n$ to first
order.
\end{Assumption}

The assumption is expected for CPU-bound verification and execution under a
cgroup quota; it fails when fixed per-process overhead (e.g.\ GC) dominates a
small~$\q$, when $\n\q$ saturates host bandwidth, or when block production is
paced by a fixed period. It is a working hypothesis with a stated domain, not a
law.

\begin{State}[$\q$-invariance]
\label{st:qinvariance}
Under Assumption~\ref{as:separable} and
$\Phi(\n,\cc)=\Tzero\,\gfun(\cc)\,\n^{-\beta}$, the OLS slope of
$\log\TPSn$ on $\log\n$ at fixed~$\cc$ does not depend on~$\q$.
\end{State}

\begin{Proof}
$\log\TPSn=\log\Phi+O(\q)$; at fixed~$\q$ the $O(\q)$ term is absorbed by the
intercept, so the slope equals $-\beta$ for every~$\q$.
\end{Proof}

Invariance is tested by a partial $F$-test on quota\,$\times\log\n$
interactions across three quota levels
(Section~\ref{sec:exp}); if rejected, the exponent is reported only on the
surviving quota interval.

\subsection{The bi-factor law and its identifiability}

More submitters~$\cc$ raise throughput until saturation; more validators~$\n$
add communication and verification, lowering it. We model the two
multiplicatively.

\begin{Def}[Bi-factor scaling law]
\label{def:bifactor}
Under the \RNM{} protocol,
\begin{equation}
\TPS(\n,\cc)=\Tzero\cdot\gfun(\cc)\cdot\n^{-\beta},
\quad
\gfun(\cc)=\frac{\cc^{\gamma}}{1+(\cc/\csat)^{\theta}},
\label{eq:bifactor}
\end{equation}
with $\Tzero>0$, $\beta>0$, $\gamma\in(0,1]$, $\theta>\gamma$, $\csat>0$.
In the unsaturated regime $\cc\ll\csat$ this is log-linear:
$\log\TPS=\log\Tzero+\gamma\log\cc-\beta\log\n$.
\end{Def}

Constraint $\beta>0$ encodes replicated-state-machine semantics; $\theta>\gamma$
makes $\gfun$ unimodal. Single-factor fits $\TPS\propto\n^{\alpha}$ can still
report $\alpha>0$ when the load generator is scaled with the network: the
regression attributes the net movement to~$\n$ alone.

\begin{Theorem}[Identifiability of the consensus exponent]
\label{thm:identifiability}
Let observations follow the unsaturated bi-factor law with additive noise of
mean zero and finite variance, along a concurrency path
$\log\cc_{i}=\lambda\log\n_{i}+\mu+\eta_{i}$
($\E[\eta_{i}\mid\n_{i}]=0$). If the empirical second moment of
$x_{i}=\log\n_{i}$ converges to a positive limit, the OLS slope $\ahat_{m}$ of
$\log\TPS_{i}$ on $x_{i}$ alone satisfies
$\ahat_{m}\xrightarrow{\Prob}\gamma\lambda-\beta$.
In particular $\ahat>0\iff\lambda>\beta/\gamma$, and $\ahat=-\beta\iff\lambda=0$.
\end{Theorem}

\begin{Proof}
Substituting the path into the log-linear law yields a simple regression with
slope $\gamma\lambda-\beta$ and mean-zero noise. The OLS slope therefore equals
that constant plus a ratio $N_{m}/D_{m}$ whose numerator converges to zero in
probability (zero-mean finite-variance summands) while the denominator converges
to a positive second moment; continuous mapping gives the claim. The sign
statements follow from $\gamma>0$.
\end{Proof}

\begin{Remark}
The bias is first-order: with $\gamma=0.8$, $\beta=0.5$ and $\lambda=1$ one
obtains $\ahat=+0.3$, and extrapolating from $\n=5$ to $\n=60$ the two exponents
differ by a factor $\approx7$, the order of the discrepancy between our earlier
studies~\cite{peniak2026a}.
\end{Remark}

\begin{Corollary}[Non-transferability]
\label{cor:sign}
Laboratories measuring along $\lambda_{1}\ne\lambda_{2}$ obtain exponents
differing by $\gamma(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})$; published exponents are
incomparable unless concurrency paths are reported.
\end{Corollary}

On a factorial grid ($\lambda=0$ by construction) we estimate
$(\log\Tzero,\gamma,\beta)$ by OLS in log-space and $(\csat,\theta)$ by
nonlinear least squares on the full $\cc$-sweep
(Section~\ref{sec:exp}).
