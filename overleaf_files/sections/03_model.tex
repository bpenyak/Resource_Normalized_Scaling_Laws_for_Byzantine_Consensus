\section{Resource-normalized measurement and the bi-factor law}
\label{sec:model}

\subsection{Resource-normalized measurement as an isolation tool}

If $\n$ validators share a host of $Q$ cores, each receives on average $Q/\n$
cores, so measured throughput mixes algorithmic cost with a shrinking compute
budget. \RNM{} is designed to isolate those effects under controlled quotas.

\begin{Def}[Resource-normalized measurement]
\label{def:rnm}
A campaign follows the \emph{\RNM{} protocol} with quota~$\q$ if every validator
container receives the same CPU quota and memory limit independently of~$\n$,
with aggregate cap $\n\q\le Q-Q_{0}$, where $Q_{0}$ is headroom for the load
generator and the OS. Normalized throughput is $\TPSn\eqdef\TPS/\q$; all
throughputs fitted below are normalized.
\end{Def}

A CFS quota is an upper bound on CPU time, not a guaranteed allocation, and
consumed CPU is not verified here; \RNM{} is therefore a controlled-cap
protocol, and what it isolates is a hypothesis tested below. With $Q=4$ and
$Q_{0}=1$ it is applied for $\n\le\resCalibNmax$ ($\n\q\le\resCalibNQ$); larger
$\n$ form a saturation study.

\begin{Assumption}[Separable compute budget]
\label{as:separable}
On the operating range of quotas in the campaign,
$\TPS(\n,\cc,\q)=\q\cdot\Phi(\n,\cc)\cdot(1+r)$, where the remainder
$r=r(\n,\cc,\q)$ satisfies $|r|\le C\q\le\tfrac12$ for a
constant~$C$ independent of~$(\n,\cc)$.
\end{Assumption}

\paragraph{What the assumption implies.}
Let $s_x$ be the standard deviation of $\log\n$ over the design. For a power-law
factor $\Phi\propto\n^{-\beta}$, the OLS slope of $\log\TPSn$ on $\log\n$ at
fixed concurrency shifts with the quota by at most $2C\q/s_x$
(Cauchy--Schwarz); it is exactly quota-free only if $r$ does not depend
on~$\n$, which the bound $|r|\le C\q$ does not imply. Quota invariance is thus
a testable property, not a consequence. Section~\ref{sec:exp} tests it across
$\q\in\{0.20,0.25,0.33\}$ and rejects it: \RNM{} stabilizes
$\beta$ at a fixed quota rather than making it quota-free. The calibrated model
is reported at $\q=0.25$; outside that regime the coefficients must be re-fit.

\begin{Def}[Bi-factor scaling characterization]
\label{def:bifactor}
Under the \RNM{} protocol,
\begin{equation}
\TPSn(\n,\cc)=\Tzero\cdot\gfun(\cc)\cdot\n^{-\beta},
\quad
\gfun(\cc)=\frac{\cc^{\gamma}}{1+(\cc/\csat)^{\theta}},
\label{eq:bifactor}
\end{equation}
with $\Tzero>0$, $\beta>0$, $\gamma\in(0,1]$, $\theta>\gamma$, $\csat>0$.
In the unsaturated regime $\cc\ll\csat$ this is log-linear:
$\log\TPSn=\log\Tzero+\gamma\log\cc-\beta\log\n$.
\end{Def}

The form separates concurrency from validator-count scaling, saturates at large
$\cc$, and contains the classical single-factor fit as the case $\gamma=0$.
We estimate $(\Tzero,\gamma,\beta)$ by OLS on the unsaturated sweep, so inside
that grid the fitted law is $\Tzero\cc^{\gamma}\n^{-\beta}$ and any curvature is
absorbed in $\gamma$; $(\csat,\theta)$ come from a separate nonlinear fit on the
full concurrency sweep, so~\eqref{eq:bifactor} is not fitted jointly.

\subsection{Identifiability}

Proposition~\ref{prop:identifiability} is the textbook \mbox{omitted-variable}
bias of regressing $\log\TPSn$ on $\log\n$ while omitting
$\log\cc$~\cite{wooldridge2010}. Its value is interpretive: it shows how to read
a measured single-factor exponent under a stated concurrency path;
Section~\ref{sec:exp} checks consistency in the evaluated deployment only.

\begin{Proposition}[Identifiability of the consensus exponent]
\label{prop:identifiability}
Let $(x_{i},\eta_{i},\varepsilon_{i})$, $i=1,\dots,m$, be independent, with
$x_{i}=\log\n_{i}$ and
$\log\cc_{i}=\lambda x_{i}+\mu+\eta_{i}$,
$\log\TPSn_{i}=\log\Tzero+\gamma\log\cc_{i}-\beta x_{i}+\varepsilon_{i}$
(unsaturated law), where $\E[\eta_{i}\mid x_{i}]=0$,
$\E[\varepsilon_{i}\mid x_{i},\eta_{i}]=0$ and the conditional variances
$\E[\eta_{i}^{2}\mid x_{i}]$, $\E[\varepsilon_{i}^{2}\mid x_{i},\eta_{i}]$ are
bounded by a constant~$M$. If the sample variance of $x_{i}$
converges to a limit $s^{2}>0$, the OLS slope $\ahat_{m}$ of
$\log\TPSn_{i}$ on $x_{i}$ alone satisfies
$\ahat_{m}\xrightarrow{\Prob}\gamma\lambda-\beta$ as $m\to\infty$.
In particular $\ahat>0\iff\lambda>\beta/\gamma$, and $\ahat=-\beta\iff\lambda=0$.
\end{Proposition}

\begin{Proof}
Substitution gives $\log\TPSn_{i}=a+\alpha x_{i}+u_{i}$ with
$\alpha=\gamma\lambda-\beta$ and $u_{i}=\varepsilon_{i}+\gamma\eta_{i}$, so
$\E[u_{i}\mid x_{i}]=0$ and $\E[u_{i}^{2}\mid x_{i}]\le\bar M\eqdef2(1+\gamma^{2})M$;
the $u_{i}$ are conditionally uncorrelated given $x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})$. The
OLS slope is $\alpha+\sum_{i}(x_{i}-\bar x)u_{i}/\sum_{i}(x_{i}-\bar x)^{2}$; the
second term has conditional mean $0$ and variance at most
$\bar M/\sum_{i}(x_{i}-\bar x)^{2}=O(1/m)$, so it vanishes in
probability by Chebyshev's inequality.
\end{Proof}

Here $m\to\infty$ means more replicated runs over the measured design, not a
wider range of~$\n$. Only a factorial design ($\lambda=0$) makes the
single-factor slope a consistent estimate of~$-\beta$.

\begin{Corollary}[Non-transferability of single-factor exponents]
\label{cor:sign}
Under the unsaturated bi-factor model, laboratories measuring along
$\lambda_{1}\ne\lambda_{2}$ obtain exponents differing by
$\gamma(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})$; without reporting~$\lambda$, single-factor
exponents should not be treated as transferable protocol properties.
\end{Corollary}
