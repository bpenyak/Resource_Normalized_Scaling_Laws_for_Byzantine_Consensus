\section{Application, implications, and limitations}
\label{sec:case}

\subsection{Application}

We instantiate Problem~\ref{prob:sizing} on $\approx3\times10^{7}$ ballots over
twelve hours ($\Dpk\approx2.1\times10^{3}$~TPS), requiring detection of a
coordinated omission fault of fraction $\phi=0.2$ within one epoch at level
$1-\epss$. Absolute demand enters via scale-up $\kappaScale$ from \RNM{} units.
With free $\kappaScale$, Problem~\ref{prob:sizing} takes
\begin{equation}
\kappaScale^{\star}
=\frac{\Dpk}{\underline{\TPSn}_{1-\epsp}(4,\cc^{\star})},
\label{eq:kappa}
\end{equation}
the minimal scale at which the lower PI bound at $\n=4$ meets $\Dpk$, yielding
$\kappaScale=\resKappa$, $\cc^{\star}=\resCstar$, and window
$[\resNminCase,\resNmaxCase]$ with $\nopt=\resNoptCase$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:window}; Figure~\ref{fig:window}).

\begin{figure}[!t]

The workflow illustrates sizing for registry and identity workloads; it is not
a prescription for binding elections on a
blockchain~\cite{springall2014,specter2020,park2021}.

\subsection{What this implies}
\label{sec:discussion}

A fitted $(\beta,\gamma)$ transfers only when concurrency path, quota regime,
and RTT class match the calibration (X2, X4). Future benchmarks should report
$\lambda$ (or state $\lambda=0$), per-validator quota, and RTT class; without
$\lambda$, single-factor exponents are not protocol properties
(Corollary~\ref{cor:sign}). \RNM{} makes factorial designs feasible on commodity
CI; durable objects are exponents, coverage, and window shape. Other mechanisms
(GC, bandwidth saturation, block-period pacing, client queues) can also move a
fitted exponent; controlling~$\lambda$ isolates one identifiable component.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\emph{Fault class.}
Encrypted transport precludes selective consensus-message injection, so X5
realizes omission and duplication only. With AUC~$\resAuc$ the safety
constraint is non-binding and throughput sets the window. This is the intended
fallback of the framework slot in Section~\ref{sec:sizing}, not a contradiction
of the sizing programme.

\emph{Range and platform.}
Commodity CI restricts physical scale to $\n\le\resNmaxMeasured$. Beyond that
range, prediction intervals quantify but do not eliminate extrapolation risk.
The bi-factor law's primary role here is to isolate~$\beta$, so that the
scale-up $\kappaScale$ in~\eqref{eq:kappa} supplies a conservative lower bound
for production capacity rather than a claim to have measured national-scale
deployments in situ. With $\beta=\resBeta$ the case-study window collapses to
$\n=\resNoptCase$. Numerical $(\beta,\gamma)$ are Besu/QBFT-specific; the
identifiability statement is not.
