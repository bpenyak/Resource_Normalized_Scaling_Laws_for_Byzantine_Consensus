\section{Application to an e-governance workload, and limitations}
\label{sec:case}

We instantiate Problem~\ref{prob:sizing} on a national-scale profile of about
$3\times10^{7}$ ballots over twelve hours (peak demand
$\Dpk\approx2.1\times10^{3}$~TPS) with the requirement that a coordinated
omission fault of fraction $\phi=0.2$ be detected within one epoch at level
$1-\epss$.

Because the law is calibrated on \RNM{}-normalized throughput, absolute demand
enters through an explicit scale-up $\kappaScale$. Taking the \emph{minimal
admissible} $\kappaScale$ (smallest value whose lower prediction bound at
$\n=4$ meets $\Dpk$) yields $\kappaScale=\resKappa$, operating concurrency
$\cc^{\star}=\resCstar$, and feasibility window
$[\resNminCase,\resNmaxCase]$ with $\nopt=\resNoptCase$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:window}); Figure~\ref{fig:window} shows the intersection.
Detector rates enter at the Youden threshold $\thr=\resDetectorThr$ of X5.

\begin{figure}[!t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.68\textwidth]{figures/fig_window}
\caption{Feasibility window at minimal admissible $\kappaScale=\resKappa$:
confidence-adjusted absolute throughput versus $\Dpk$; shaded band
$[\nmin,\nmax]=[\resNminCase,\resNmaxCase]$}
\label{fig:window}
\end{figure}

The window is most sensitive to $\beta$ (shifts $\log\nmax$), to $\rho$
(inflates $\nmin$ via~\eqref{eq:nmin}), to RTT-elevated $\beta$ from X4, and to
tightening $\epsp$ or $\epss$ (closing from both sides). Operationally: with
confidence $1-\epsp$ the network sustains the peak up to $\nmax$, detects the
specified fault from $\nmin$ upward, and the cheapest admissible configuration
is $\nopt$ at production scale $\kappaScale$.

\begin{Remark}
This illustrates the sizing workflow; it is not a recommendation to run binding
elections on a blockchain~\cite{springall2014,specter2020,park2021}. The same
workflow applies to registry and identity workloads.
\end{Remark}

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\emph{Fault class.} Encrypted transport precludes selective consensus-message
injection, so X5 realizes omission and duplication only. Signer-metrics
detection is weak (AUC~$\resAuc$), the safety constraint is non-binding at the
operating point, and the window is set by throughput.

\emph{Absolute TPS and range.} \RNM{} quotas keep absolute throughput far below
dedicated-hardware benchmarks; every claim concerns exponents, coverage and
sizing. Absolute demand enters only through $\kappaScale$. The largest measured
set is $\resNmaxMeasured$; beyond it, intervals of Statement~\ref{st:pi}
quantify but do not eliminate extrapolation risk. With $\beta=\resBeta$ the
window collapses to $\n=\resNoptCase$ --- a substantive conclusion, not an
artefact.

\emph{Single host and platform.} Theorem~\ref{thm:identifiability} is about
least squares and is platform-independent; the calibrated $(\beta,\gamma)$ are
not. Cross-client replication (GoQuorum/X9) is deliberately out of scope, so
all numerical coefficients are Besu/QBFT-specific. Exact $\q$-invariance is
rejected on $\{0.20,0.25,0.33\}$, and the fit is reported at $\q=0.25$ only.
Structural claims (bias form, feasible-set shape) are the durable part;
numerical values are provisional.
