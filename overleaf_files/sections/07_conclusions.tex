\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

\paragraph{What is proved.}
Along a concurrency path of slope~$\lambda$, the single-factor OLS scaling
exponent satisfies $\ahat_{m}\xrightarrow{\Prob}\gamma\lambda-\beta$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:identifiability}). Opposing monotone constraints yield a
closed integer feasibility window (Proposition~\ref{prop:window}).

\paragraph{What is confirmed experimentally.}
Under \RNM{} on public four-core CI, the fit yields
$\beta=\resBeta\pm\resBetaSE$, $\gamma=\resGamma\pm\resGammaSE$, and path
re-sampling tracks $\ahat_{m}\xrightarrow{\Prob}\gamma\lambda-\beta$.
$\q$-invariance fails on the full tested range; prediction-interval coverage
meets nominal levels. With AUC$\,=\,\resAuc$ the window falls back to the
throughput bound. Emulated WAN RTT raises~$\beta$ from $\resBetaRttZero$ to
$\resBetaRttHigh$, so LAN-calibrated capacity models are optimistic for
geo-distributed deployments.

\paragraph{What remains open.}
Equivocation-capable instrumentation; multi-client numerical checks; latency or
finality constraints inside the window; online recalibration of~$\beta$.

The principal outcome of this work is not a new benchmark coefficient, but an
identifiability result: concurrency-path design fundamentally determines how
measured scaling exponents may be interpreted.
