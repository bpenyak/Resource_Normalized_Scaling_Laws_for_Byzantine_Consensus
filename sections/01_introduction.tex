\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Permissioned Byzantine Fault Tolerant (BFT) consensus underpins e-governance
systems --- voting, registries, digital identity --- and raises a concrete
procurement question: \emph{how many validators should the network have?}

The validator count~$\n$ pulls in opposite directions. Throughput argues for
small~$\n$: every validator executes every transaction, and PBFT-family
protocols exchange $O(\n^{2})$ messages per block. Detection reliability argues
for large~$\n$. This paper develops a capacity-planning methodology that
combines the two under resource-normalized measurement.

Published single-factor fits $\TPS(\n)\propto\n^{\alpha}$ disagree in magnitude
and sometimes in sign. Our earlier studies, extrapolated to sixty validators,
differ by roughly an order of magnitude. Such disagreement is often read as
protocol-specific behaviour. Within the investigated setting we show that it
can arise from measurement design alone: concurrency is rarely held fixed
while~$\n$ varies, so the fitted scaling exponent mixes load-generator growth
with consensus overhead. Without reporting the concurrency path, published
exponents are difficult to interpret as intrinsic protocol properties.
Packing many validators onto one host adds a second confound --- CPU
contention --- that resource-normalized measurement is designed to isolate.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
Methodologically, we introduce a resource-normalized measurement (\RNM{})
protocol that fixes CPU quota~$\q$ per validator and reports
$\TPSn=\TPS/\q$, separating consensus cost from host contention.
Analytically, under the unsaturated bi-factor model, along a concurrency path
of slope~$\lambda$ the single-factor OLS fit satisfies
$\ahat_{m}\xrightarrow{\Prob}\gamma\lambda-\beta$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:identifiability}); the relation is a consequence of the
model assumptions, not an empirical universal. From a planning perspective, we
formulate chance-constrained sizing whose feasible set is $[\nmin,\nmax]$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:window}). Empirically, we check prediction-interval
coverage and exercise the workflow in a public Besu/QBFT CI campaign
(Sections~\ref{sec:exp}--\ref{sec:case}), including a path check of consistency
with Proposition~\ref{prop:identifiability} within that configuration.
Scope: throughput and detection under the investigated deployment; absolute TPS
are environment-specific; cross-client replication is out of scope. Reported
coefficients are deployment-specific; the methodology is intended to transfer
under matching measurement conditions.
