\section{Experimental validation}
\label{sec:exp}

\subsection{Setup and design}

The campaign runs in a public continuous-integration workflow on a four-core,
16~GB hosted runner. Each job provisions a Hyperledger Besu~\cite{besu} QBFT
network of~$\n$ validators under the \RNM{} protocol
(Definition~\ref{def:rnm}). A closed-loop generator drives~$\cc$ concurrent
submitters and records steady-state committed throughput after warm-up. Faults
are injected with kernel traffic control (omission and duplication; not
equivocation). Detector rates come from signer metrics over~$\w$ rounds.

\textbf{X1}/\textbf{X3} calibrate $(\beta,\gamma)$ on a factorial grid;
\textbf{X2} tests $\q$-invariance; \textbf{X3b} re-samples along concurrency
paths; \textbf{X4} varies emulated RTT; \textbf{X5} supplies detector rates;
\textbf{X7}/\textbf{X8} check prediction-interval coverage and sizing ablation.
Cross-client replication (\textbf{X9}/GoQuorum) is out of scope. In total
$\resTotalRuns$ successful records, $\approx\resCoreHours$ core-hours,
$\n_{\max}=\resNmaxMeasured$.

\paragraph{Reproducibility.}
All experiments are fully reproducible from the public CI workflow and archived
artefacts at \url{https://github.com/bpenyak/paper_3}.

\subsection{Results}

\emph{X1/X3 --- bi-factor calibration.}
Joint OLS on the reference-quota subset yields
$\beta=\resBeta\pm\resBetaSE$ and $\gamma=\resGamma\pm\resGammaSE$
($R^{2}=\resRsq$; Table~\ref{tab:results}). Under \RNM{} one expects $\beta>0$:
consensus cost grows with validator count when concurrency is held fixed. The
fit confirms that expectation at $\q=0.25$.

\emph{X3b --- path check of Theorem~\ref{thm:identifiability}.}
A single-factor fit along $\lambda=1$ gives $\ahat=\resAlphaHat$ against the
predicted $\gamma\lambda-\beta=\resAlphaPred$.
Figure~\ref{fig:confounding} shows that measured exponents track the line
$\gamma\lambda-\beta$ across $\lambda\in\{0,0.5,1,2\}$. The protocol need not
change for the observed scaling exponent to change; only the concurrency
schedule does.

\emph{X2 --- $\q$-invariance.}
As anticipated under Assumption~\ref{as:separable}, invariance is a restricted-
regime property rather than a universal one. The partial $F$-test rejects it on
the full set $\{0.20,0.25,0.33\}$ ($p=\resQinvP$): $\beta$ drops from
$\approx\resBetaQmid$ at $\q=0.25$ to $\resBetaQhigh$ at $\q=0.33$. Following
the protocol of Section~\ref{sec:model}, coefficients are reported at the
reference quota $\q=0.25$ only.

\emph{X4 --- latency.}
Under elevated emulated RTT, $\beta$ rises from $\resBetaRttZero$ to
$\resBetaRttHigh$. WAN delay steepens the consensus penalty in validator count.
Sizing rules calibrated on LAN-like RTT are optimistic for geo-distributed
deployments.

\emph{X5 --- detector.}
Signer-metric detection of omission/duplication yields AUC$\,=\,\resAuc$
(Figure~\ref{fig:roc}). Under encrypted transport this network-layer detector
is essentially uninformative, so $\nmin$ is non-binding. Rather than a failure
of the sizing framework, that outcome is evidence of its resilience: when
detection contributes no information, the feasible set falls back safely to
being bounded entirely by the identifiable throughput exponent. A stronger
detector would raise~$\nmin$ without changing the mathematics of
Section~\ref{sec:sizing}.

\emph{X7/X8 --- coverage and ablation.}
Hold-out coverage meets the nominal levels in Table~\ref{tab:results}.
The fitted covariance matrix confirms that $\hat{V}$ remains monotonic on the
extrapolation range $\n\ge\resNmaxMeasured$, satisfying the hypothesis of
Lemma~\ref{lem:detequiv}; on the measured grid $\n\le\resNmaxMeasured$ we
evaluate~$h$ directly. Out-of-sample log-RMSE for linear / single-factor /
USL~\cite{gunther2007} / bi-factor predictors is $\resRmseLinear$,
$\resRmseSingle$, $\resRmseUsl$, $\resRmseOurs$; relative sizing costs are
$\resCostLinear$, $\resCostSingle$, $\resCostUsl$, $\resCostOurs$. USL
underperforms here because it conflates concurrency with replica count into a
single load variable, whereas the bi-factor law separates them.
Section~\ref{sec:case} instantiates the resulting window.
