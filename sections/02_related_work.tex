\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\emph{BFT protocols.} Classical Practical Byzantine Fault
Tolerance~\cite{castro1999} tolerates $f<\n/3$ faulty replicas at $O(\n^{2})$
messages per decision, on the foundations of~\cite{lamport1982,dwork1988}.
HotStuff~\cite{yin2019} reduces this to $O(\n)$; Tendermint~\cite{buchman2016}
gives instant finality; Algorand~\cite{gilad2017} and
HoneyBadgerBFT~\cite{miller2016} employ randomization;
Vukoli\'{c}~\cite{vukolic2015} argues that no protocol dominates across
operating regimes. All fix the validator set at deployment time and none
supplies a procedure for choosing its size.

\emph{Benchmarking.} Empirical characterizations of permissioned ledgers include
Hyperledger Fabric~\cite{androulaki2018,thakkar2018},
BLOCKBENCH~\cite{dinh2017} and the BFT-SMaRt ordering service~\cite{sousa2018}.
They report throughput at a handful of fixed configurations, do not separate
offered load from validator count --- exactly the confounding formalized in
Theorem~\ref{thm:identifiability} --- and do not extrapolate.

\emph{Scaling laws.} Amdahl~\cite{amdahl1967} and Gustafson~\cite{gustafson1988}
bound speedup under a fixed serial fraction. Gunther's Universal Scalability
Law~\cite{gunther2007} adds a quadratic coherency term,
$C(\n)=\n/(1+\sigma(\n-1)+\kappa\n(\n-1))$, and is the closest existing model to
ours: it describes capacity as a function of the number of workers sharing one
workload, but does not separate the offered-concurrency dimension and has not
been calibrated for BFT consensus. We use it as a baseline in
Section~\ref{sec:exp}.

\emph{Statistics and optimization.} Hoeffding's inequality~\cite{hoeffding1963}
and the concentration toolkit~\cite{boucheron2013} bound the probability that a
faulty minority escapes detection; predictive intervals follow the delta
method~\cite{seber2003} and the residual bootstrap~\cite{efron1993}.
Chance-constrained programming is due to Charnes and Cooper~\cite{charnes1959},
with convex approximations surveyed in~\cite{nemirovski2007}. The formulation is
standard and we use it as such; what is specific to validator-set sizing is the
opposing monotonicity of the two constraints, which turns the feasible set into
a closed interval instead of a half-line.

\emph{E-governance.} Security analyses of deployed remote-voting
systems~\cite{springall2014,specter2020} and the assessment in~\cite{park2021}
establish that consensus-layer guarantees resolve neither endpoint nor coercion
threats. Our earlier work developed a single-factor throughput
calibration~\cite{peniak2026a} and an adaptive consensus-switching mechanism
with detection-based bounds~\cite{peniak2026b}. This paper unifies and corrects
both: it identifies the confounding that inflated the exponent
of~\cite{peniak2026a} and supplies the detector characterization
that~\cite{peniak2026b} assumed.
